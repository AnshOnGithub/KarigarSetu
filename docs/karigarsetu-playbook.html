Part A

Reading the problem statement

The problem statement asks for three AI features (image studio, multilingual cataloguer, pricing assistant) and a goal: connect artisans "directly with larger B2B buyers or government e-marketplaces". It never says which marketplaces, who legally sells, or how a listing technically reaches a buyer. Most teams will build the three features and draw an arrow labelled "ONDC / GeM". Judges will probe that arrow. Winning means answering it concretely.

What the problem statement leaves open

Open questionWhy it mattersOur resolution
Which marketplaces?Each has different onboarding, data fields and technical access.Don't bet on one. Build a channel-neutral master catalogue with adapters: ONDC, GeM, B2B buyers, direct storefront.
How does data get there?GeM has no public third-party listing API that we could find; ONDC needs a registered seller app.Four publishing rails, each using the route that actually exists today (see below).
Who is the seller?ONDC and GeM onboarding need GSTIN, PAN and bank details. Most target artisans don't have a GSTIN.An aggregator is seller of record (scheme corporation, cluster, SHG, producer company); KarigarSetu manages the catalogue for each artisan under it.
"Market trends" from where?A pricing "ML model" with no market data is a guess.Price = cost floor + comparable-listings band + model; artisan always edits. Data from channel feeds and partner orders.
Which languages?Input "regional languages"; output "English and Hindi".Voice and UI in 8 languages; listing generated in English + Hindi + the artisan's own language (read back aloud).
Offline expectations?Rural connectivity; AI needs network.Capture offline, queue AI and publishing, sync automatically on reconnect. Never claim AI runs offline.
Who runs the backend?A government-supported beneficiary programme needs an operator.Ministry or its corporation operates the hub; platforms integrate with it (a digital public infrastructure pattern).
Orders, payment, delivery?A listing without fulfilment doesn't raise income.Orders flow back to the app via API; payment and logistics are handled by the channel (ONDC network, GeM, buyer) or the aggregator.
How is impact measured?Impact goal is "increase average annual income".Ministry dashboard: artisans onboarded, live listings, orders, GMV and income by district and scheme.

Design decisions this leads to

  1. Catalogue as infrastructure, not a marketplace. KarigarSetu does not compete with GeM or ONDC buyer apps. It produces clean, verified, bilingual product data and pushes it everywhere.
  2. One master record per product. Channel-specific formats (ONDC catalogue, GeM sheet, B2B JSON) are derived, never hand-maintained.
  3. Pull and push. Platforms can poll an incremental feed or receive signed webhooks the moment an artisan publishes, edits or delists.
  4. Artisan always approves. AI drafts, the artisan confirms by listening to it in their language.
  5. Aggregator model for legal selling, so a first-time smartphone user without a GSTIN can still sell.
  6. Honest about access. Where no API exists (GeM today), the system generates the exact upload file; direct integration follows an MoU.

Verdict on "end output as an API"

Right foundation, not the whole answer. An API only publishes when a platform consumes it. So the deliverable is the API plus a concrete route into each channel, a seller-of-record model, and a demo that shows data landing somewhere.

Seller of record: the piece most teams miss

Onboarding on ONDC and GeM needs GSTIN, PAN, bank account and address. A first-time smartphone user can't do that alone. We propose:

  • Aggregator sells, artisan earns. The ministry's corporations (NSFDC, NBCFDC, NSKFDC), a craft cluster, an SHG federation or a producer company registers once on each channel.
  • Each artisan is a sub-profile with beneficiary ID, Udyam number (optional) and bank account. Every product carries the artisan's identity to the buyer (name, craft, district).
  • Payments settle to the aggregator and pass to the artisan's account, with a ledger visible in the app.
  • As an artisan grows (own GSTIN), they can graduate to selling directly. The catalogue moves with them.
Verify before the finale

Channel rules change. Confirm with ONDC and GeM helpdesks: current seller onboarding requirements, whether GeM offers any partner/API programme, and ONDC category codes for handicrafts. Put the confirmed answer in the deck.

What makes this hard to beat

Other public SIH26090 repos cover the three AI features. These are the angles most don't cover:

  1. A working integration API with docs, not an arrow on a slide. Show Swagger at /docs.
  2. Live demo moment: tap Publish on the phone → the product appears on a mock "Government Portal" page within seconds via webhook. Build this.
  3. Seller-of-record model tied to the sponsor ministry's own corporations.
  4. Fair-to-storefront QR: directly answers the PS background about physical exhibitions.
  5. Listen-back approval in the artisan's language, so a non-reader can verify what AI wrote.
  6. Ministry impact dashboard measuring the PS's own impact goal (income).

Judge questions: prepared answers

Why not just use India Handmade Bazaar, Amazon Karigar or Flipkart Samarth?
Those are single marketplaces with their own onboarding. KarigarSetu is the catalogue layer before them: voice-first creation for low-literacy users, then publishing to many channels including those.
Does GeM have an API you integrate with?
We found no public third-party listing API. Today the hub generates GeM-ready bulk data and the aggregator, as the registered seller, uploads it. Direct integration is a partnership step, and the adapter is isolated so adding it doesn't change the app.
How is the listing actually live on ONDC?
Through a seller-side network participant. Our hub already outputs the ONDC retail catalogue shape; the connector serves it on search requests. For the finale, demonstrate on ONDC's staging environment or a partner seller app.
The artisan has no GSTIN. Who sells?
The aggregator — a scheme corporation, cluster or SHG — is seller of record. The artisan's identity travels with every product and payment is passed through to their bank account.
What if AI writes something wrong?
AI only uses facts from the photo and the artisan's words (enforced in the prompt), and every listing is read back aloud in the artisan's language before publishing. Nothing publishes without approval.
Is the price suggestion really "machine learning"?
Be precise. Prototype: cost floor + AI market estimate. Production: retrieval of comparable listings from channel data plus a gradient-boosted model, clamped to never go below cost. The artisan always sets the final price.
What does it cost to run?
Roughly ₹3 per studio photo at Gemini's paid image rate (~$0.039), plus text and speech on low-cost tiers. Self-hosting open models reduces this at scale. Operated by the corporation as part of beneficiary support.
What about privacy of voice and photos?
Explicit consent, India-hosted data, no voice stored after transcription unless the artisan keeps it, deletion on request — aligned with the DPDP Act 2023. Keys move to the server before release.
How will you measure impact?
Dashboard by district and scheme: time-to-list, live listings, orders, GMV, repeat buyers, and self-reported income at 0, 6 and 12 months.

Artisan flow

  1. Photo. Camera or gallery, square crop, normalised to ≤1600 px JPEG, copied into app storage.
  2. Studio. Plain white generates automatically. Other looks on tap: In a home, Top view, Close-up, Festive gift. Hold the photo to compare with the original. Advanced (collapsed): write a custom scene; rotate, flip, crop (1:1, 4:5, 4:3); brightness, contrast, colour, warmth.
  3. Voice. Tap to record, speak in own language, transcript editable.
  4. Listing. AI writes English title + description, Hindi title + description, 3 highlights, category, and a version in the artisan's language that is read aloud.
  5. Price. Material cost, hours × hourly rate, profit 20/30/40 %. AI market estimate shown alongside. Artisan sets final price and stock.
  6. Publish. Choose ONDC / GeM / B2B. Publish or save draft. Listing syncs to the hub.
  7. Manage. Edit price, stock, channels; delist. Changes sync. Orders move New → Accepted → Packed → Shipped → Delivered.

AI pipeline

Image studio

  • Model: gemini-2.5-flash-image (configurable). remove.bg fallback for white only.
  • Prompt rules: remove background, hands and clutter; place in the chosen scene; keep the product exactly as is (shape, colours, patterns, weave, imperfections); correct lighting and white balance; square output, no text.
  • Custom prompts are wrapped in the same product-fidelity rules.
  • Colour edits use a Skia colour matrix, applied live and baked to JPEG on "Use this photo".

Speech and listing

  • Speech-to-text: Sarvam saarika:v2.5 when configured, otherwise Gemini audio transcription in the artisan's script.
  • Listing: structured JSON schema output (title, description, title_hi, description_hi, local_description, highlights, category, suggested price, price reason). System prompt forbids inventing materials, sizes or certifications and asks for SEO-friendly titles.
  • No network or no key: listing built from the artisan's own words so the flow never blocks.

Pricing engine

Today Partly built: suggested price = round₁₀((material + hours × rate) × (1 + profit %)). The AI adds a market estimate with a one-line reason in the artisan's language.

Target design Planned:

  1. Cost floor — material + labour. Never suggest below this.
  2. Comparable band — embed the photo and title; retrieve similar handmade items from channel feeds and completed orders; take the 25th–75th percentile price.
  3. Model — gradient-boosted regressor on category, craft, material, size, hours, region, channel and image embedding; trained on hub order data as it accumulates.
  4. Output — a range and a recommended point, clamped to ≥ cost floor, with a spoken reason. Artisan decides.

Offline & sync

  • All products, orders, profile and settings persist on the phone; the app works without network for browsing and editing.
  • Publishing marks a product pending and pushes to the hub. Failure marks failed; on reconnect every pending or failed product is re-sent.
  • Re-sends use the local id as clientId, so the hub updates rather than duplicates.
  • Product detail shows sync state: sending / sent to marketplaces / will retry.
  • Planned queue photo studio and listing generation while offline and run them on reconnect.

Security & privacy

  • Partner keys, artisan tokens and admin key checked server-side; secrets stored hashed; admin key compared in constant time.
  • Webhooks signed with per-subscription secrets; SSRF guard rejects private hosts in production.
  • Input validated with Zod; image type and size limits (8 MB).
  • Before release AI provider keys are currently bundled in the app for the prototype — move all AI calls behind the hub.
  • Planned phone OTP login, consent screen, India-hosted storage, data deletion on request (DPDP Act 2023).
